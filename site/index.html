My-Domain

Your devices. Your domain.

my-domain links your own phone and computers so they work as one set: files, clipboard, chat, notifications, remote control and more. They talk directly, end-to-end encrypted. A small registry server only introduces them.

Everything that moves between your devices

One app on each device, one shared Rust core underneath. Every feature below is built and in the code today.

  • Files and folders

    Send batches that pause and resume, browse another device’s shared folders, keep folders in sync. Big files go over Wi-Fi Direct or the phone’s hotspot when you’re near.

  • Clipboard

    Copy here, paste there, pictures too, with a history on both apps. Passwords copied from a password manager stay on the device.

  • Chat

    Encrypted messages between your own devices, kept across restarts.

  • Notifications

    The phone’s notifications on the computer: reply, tap actions, dismiss on either side. Verification codes with one-click Copy, off until you turn it on.

  • Remote control

    See the phone’s screen on the computer and tap it, see a computer’s screen on the phone, or use the phone as a touchpad and keyboard.

  • Share mouse and keyboard

    Move one pointer across your Macs, Windows PCs and Linux computers, with a hotkey that always takes control back.

  • Find and ring

    Ring a device at full volume, find your phone or computer on a map, or put a lost phone in Lost mode with your message.

  • Audio and calls

    Phone audio on the computer and computer sound on the phone. Answer phone calls on the computer over Bluetooth hands-free.

  • Notes

    A shared notepad on all your devices, with reminders and a phone widget.

  • Timers

    Start a timer on one device; the devices you pick ring when it ends.

  • Read later

    A list of links, the same on every device. Nothing is fetched from the web.

  • Automations

    When a device connects, runs low or wakes, do something. Plus schedules, your own commands as buttons, and local webhooks.

      How it works

      The server introduces your devices. After that, they talk to each other.

      1. The registry introduces

        Each device signs in and lists its addresses. The server tells your devices about each other, and nothing more. Devices on the same network also find each other with no server at all.

      2. FCM wakes a sleeping phone

        No always-on socket draining the battery: when a device is needed, a free Firebase Cloud Messaging push wakes it.

      3. They connect directly

        QUIC on the local network, then IPv6, port mapping, hole punching and WebRTC, cheapest first. If nothing direct works, the server relays traffic it can’t read, and a faster route takes over when one opens.

      4. Everything is encrypted end to end

        Your passphrase becomes a key with Argon2id; messages are sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 and signed by the device that sent them.

      Private by design

      The server is treated as untrusted. Your devices decide what reaches them.

      What the server knows

      • Your username and your list of devices, with their names and network addresses
      • When devices are online, and when one wakes another

      What it never sees

      • Your messages, files, clipboard, notifications, screens, audio or locations
      • Your passphrase or your device key
      • A stored copy of anything you sent

      • A key for every device

        Remembered the first time two devices meet. Compare fingerprints or a safety number to verify; a changed key is refused until you trust it.

      • Add a device by QR code

        A one-time link that works for 5 minutes and never contains your password.

      • Off until you allow it

        Control, seeing the screen and taking over mouse and keyboard are allowed one device at a time, and asked for when first used.

      • Remove a lost device

        A signed removal reaches all your devices. Change the passphrase on all of them at once to shut it out for good.

      Read the November 2026 security review, with what is fixed and what is still open, and the privacy help page.

      Platforms and download

      Installers are built by the App release workflow and attached to GitHub Releases.

      GitHub Releases

      • macOS

        .dmg for Apple silicon and Intel

        Some features ask for Accessibility, Screen Recording or Local Network permission.

      • Windows

        -setup.exe or .msi, x64

        Files, clipboard, phone notifications, mouse and keyboard sharing and more. A few features aren’t offered on Windows yet.

      • Linux

        .AppImage or .deb, x86_64

        X11 and Wayland (through the desktop portals). Needs WebKitGTK 4.1: Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 37 or newer.

      • Android

        .apk, Android 7.0 or newer

        The phone side of almost everything: notifications, screen, camera, radios, find and ring, widgets and Quick Settings tiles.

      The builds aren’t code-signed yet, so macOS and Windows warn the first time you open them. How to open them, and what each file is.

      Nothing on the Releases page yet? Build it yourself: the README has the commands.

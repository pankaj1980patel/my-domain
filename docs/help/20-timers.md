Timers count down on all your devices at once. Start one on your computer while you cook, and your phone shows it too; choose which devices ring when it ends. Pause it, add a minute or stop it from whichever device is nearest.

## Start a timer

- **On the computer:** choose **Timers** in the sidebar (or press **Cmd+K**, **Ctrl+K** on Windows and Linux, and type **Timers**; the menu bar panel has a **Timers** button too).
- **On the phone:** open **Timers** from the Devices screen.

Give it a label if you like ("Pasta"), then tap one of the quick lengths (1, 3, 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, or an hour), or type hours, minutes and seconds and choose **Start**. A timer can be a second to a week long.

Under **Ring on**, tick the devices that should ring when it ends. The device you start it on is ticked to begin with. Only devices whose app has timers are listed; a device that's off or out of reach still shows the timer and rings when it gets the timer in time.

## While it runs

Every device with the app shows the timer counting down, with what's left and when it ends. On the phone it also shows in a notification, so you see it on the lock screen.

- **Pause** stops the countdown on every device; **Resume** carries on from where it was.
- **+1 min** adds a minute.
- **Change** next to "Rings on" picks other devices to ring.
- **Cancel** removes it everywhere.

## When it ends

The devices you picked ring: the computer brings its window forward with a chime and a card, the phone rings with your alarm sound (even while it's silenced for notifications, as an alarm would). **Stop** on any device stops it ringing on all of them. **+1 min** gives it another minute, and **Start again** runs it once more for as long as it was set for. Devices that weren't picked just show "Time's up".

The computer chimes for up to a minute; the card stays until you stop the timer. A timer that ended while a device was off or asleep doesn't ring there when it's back if that was over ten minutes ago, but it still shows as ended until it's stopped (for up to a day).

## Good to know

- Timers go straight between your devices over the same encrypted links as everything else. Nothing is kept on a server.
- Each device counts down by its own clock, which your phone and computer keep right by themselves. A device whose clock is set wrong by hand will be off by as much.
- **On the phone**, a timer rings on time only if the app may set exact alarms (Android 12 and later ask you once: **Settings → Apps → my-domain → Alarms & reminders**). Without that it may ring a few minutes late. The Timers screen says so, with a button to allow it.
- A device with an app from before timers doesn't show them; update it to see them there.

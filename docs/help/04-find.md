You can ask where your phone is from a computer, or where a computer is from your phone. The device being found always decides: nothing is shared until you allow it there, and it tells you each time.

## Find my phone

1. On the phone, open Settings and turn on **Let my computers see where this phone is**. It is off until you do.
2. The phone also needs Android's location permission, and Location turned on.
3. On the computer, open the phone's page and press **Locate**. You see the position, how accurate it is and how old, and **Open map**.

Each time it is shared, the phone shows a notification saying who asked. It is only sent when asked, never in the background. If sharing is off, the computer is told so and nothing is sent.

## Find my computer

1. On the computer, open Settings and turn on **Let my phone see where this computer is**. It is off until you do.
2. On the phone, open the computer's page and use **Locate**.

The computer asks its own system for the position:

- **macOS** asks you once for location access.
- **Windows** needs Location services and "Let desktop apps access your location" on, in Settings → Privacy & security → Location.
- **Linux** uses GeoClue (your desktop's Location Services switch applies). If GeoClue isn't installed, it can't answer.

A computer has no GPS, so its position comes from nearby Wi-Fi and is less precise than a phone's.
